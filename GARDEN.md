# Moss around the machinery

Sprig should feel like a place someone tends: useful, a little peculiar, with room for curiosity, warmth and play. Small acts of care belong in the things people encounter repeatedly: an address, a corner tab, sharing a program, finding yesterday’s draft intact. Restraint is part of that care.

After a substantial stretch of implementation, take a short visit to the workbench. Run something small. Open and close the editor. Notice the address. Ask:

- Does this still feel like a place we want to spend time in?
- What small act of care belongs here?

It might be a clearer error, fewer words, a preserved draft, or a playful detail. A new feature is optional.

## A first resident

**A little fern** is ordinary Moss source in `src/examples.ts`. The interpreter has no special fern operation. Copying and joining strings makes its leaflets; terminal input gives it a name and paces three stages of unfurling. The self-contained and grafted versions share the same words and visit. Tests run both, including the graft through the real local store and worker bridge.

Change the `left`, `right`, and `spine` words in the self-contained example to grow another shape. Keep examples we are fond of as the runtime develops: they can also tell us when an intended language contract has changed.

## Seeds for later

The hash garden remains a design study: an 8 × 8 grid of hex digits in an 8 × 8 pixel font, a 16-colour palette, and complementary foreground/background indices `n` and `n XOR 15`. Seasonal a–f pictures make a small artistic challenge. A bounded local search could look for pleasing hashes by varying an ignored source comment.

The fuller fern world will need graphical UI and the Sprig runtime. The resident here is a terminal sketch that can already be edited, run, published and grafted.
